The Models

Outcome

Both models predict azoospermia: no spermatozoa in the ejaculate, confirmed on at least two semen analyses with centrifugation of the pellet. The question they answer is whether a man presenting for infertility evaluation can be told his probability of azoospermia from a blood test, before or instead of a semen analysis.

Development

Tradewell et al. (2022) developed both models on 946 men evaluated at the University of Miami, after excluding prior vasectomy, solitary testis, exogenous testosterone or anabolic steroid use, and Y chromosome microdeletions. They then validated the models on 1,955 men from Milan (IRCCS Ospedale San Raffaele) and 596 from Chicago (University of Illinois).

FSH-only quadratic

The Miami men were binned by FSH and the observed proportion azoospermic in each bin was fit with a second-degree polynomial (Fig. 1 of the paper, R² = 0.95):

\[p(\%) = 0.133\,\mathrm{FSH}^2 - 0.965\,\mathrm{FSH} + 10.1\]

The curve is U-shaped: 10.1% at FSH 0, a minimum of 8.35% at FSH 3.63 IU/L, and 100% at 29.9 IU/L. azoospermia_fsh divides by 100 and clips to [0, 1] by default.

What the reproduction shows

The notebook Reproducing Tradewell et al. 2022 rebuilds the paper from the public deposit. In short:

  • The cohort sizes (946 / 1,955 / 596) are reproduced exactly, with one departure from the stated exclusion rule (see The Data).

  • A fresh logistic fit on the Miami rows recovers every Table 3 coefficient within one standard error.

  • The external AUCs reproduce: Milan 0.786 / 0.786 and Chicago 0.718 / 0.707 (logistic / FSH-only) against the paper’s 0.79 / 0.78 and 0.72 / 0.71.

  • Calibration, which the paper did not report, is where the models differ. The logistic model under-predicts in Milan (calibration-in-the-large +0.52 on the log-odds scale, slope 0.73); the FSH-only quadratic is markedly over-confident on both sites (slope 0.23 to 0.34), and because it saturates at exactly 0 and 1 it cannot be recalibrated on the logit scale.

  • An intercept-only recalibration fixes calibration-in-the-large per site. A logistic model in FSH alone, logit(p) = −3.17 + 0.145·FSH fit on Miami, matches the quadratic’s discrimination and Brier score with far better calibration. That model is this reproduction’s proposal, not part of the published paper, and is planned as a separately named function in a later release.

Discrimination

The paper reports AUCs of 0.79 (logistic) and 0.78 (FSH-only) in Milan and 0.72 and 0.71 in Chicago. tests/test_azoospermia.py checks the library against these when data/processed/ is present.

What is not implemented

  • Refit (full-precision) coefficients. Recovered in the notebook but kept out of the package so that the package stays an implementation of the published equation.

  • Risk thresholds. The paper does not define a cutoff for “high risk”, so the package reports probabilities only.

  • Obstructive vs non-obstructive azoospermia. The outcome is any azoospermia; the models do not distinguish cause.
